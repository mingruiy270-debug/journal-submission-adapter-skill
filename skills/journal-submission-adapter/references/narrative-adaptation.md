# Narrative Adaptation

## Establish The Contribution

State the manuscript's question and answer in one or two sentences using its own evidence. Identify the original contribution: an original dataset, an experimentally controlled comparison, a useful biological distinction, an analytical advance, or a well-supported integration. A list of technologies is not the central contribution.

Build a compact claim-to-evidence map for the principal claims, not an exhaustive new audit database:

| Claim | Source and unit | Measurement/inference | Key result and effective n | Figure/table | Wording strength |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Keep discrepancies visible in working notes. If locked graphics conflict with statistical source results, ask the author to resolve the conflict and do not relabel it silently. Narrative work is not permission to select whichever statistic produces a better story.

## Transfer Function, Not Text

For each selected pattern ask: what does it do for the reader, and can the manuscript honestly do that? Examples:

- Start with a disease-relevant molecular question rather than the tool stack.
- Move the defining observation before secondary method diagnostics.
- Separate a human observation from a controlled model that tests a related question.
- Use a measured tissue phenotype to anchor an expression programme, without asserting the entire causal chain.
- End with a specific biological implication proportional to the experiments.

Do not mimic distinctive wording, repeat another paper's hypotheses as the author's discovery, or claim a rescue/mechanism that was not tested. Structural similarity does not require new citations.

## Section Work

Title: name the subject and distinguish the contribution. Choose descriptive or claim-oriented phrasing based on actual comparators and evidence. Do not add untested causal verbs to sound like the journal.

Abstract: lead with the question, not methods inventory. Give the decisive observations and necessary numbers. Separate different endpoints and designs. Follow the official structured/unstructured rule and word limit. Define essential abbreviations; remove those used once. Closing interpretation should answer the opening question.

In a structured abstract, keep each section's job: Background establishes the problem/question; Methods states the design; Results gives the findings; Conclusions interprets the answer. Finding-first Results and cover-letter writing does not mean moving results into the Background subsection.

Introduction: establish the biological problem, identify the specific unresolved relation and explain why this design can address it. Keep general background short. Do not declare every technique a research gap.

Results: choose a sequence of scientific questions. Each subsection starts with the finding, provides the evidence and ends with the next logical question only where a transition is useful. Move diagnostics or duplicate displays to supplementary material only within the authorized scope; retain results that materially bound the main interpretation.

Discussion: explain the advance, compare it to direct prior evidence, address source-specific differences and identify what follows from the study. Discuss a candidate as a candidate. Concentrate substantive limitations in a focused passage; retain local qualifiers when needed to prevent a factual overclaim.

Cover letter: address the editor's readership, the unresolved question and what the study adds. Include required declarations and confidential access details only where appropriate. Published article prose can inform framing, but cover letters are rarely public; do not claim to have reviewed unpublished letters. Do not explain previous desk rejections unless the journal asks.

## Sentence And Paragraph Pass

Lead with the core judgement, then give the reason and evidence. Preserve qualifications that affect causality, population, measurement or statistical interpretation. Delete caveats responding only to an imagined objection. Use varied, natural sentence lengths, with one main proposition per sentence. Avoid a series of parallel long sentences and repetitive "not X but Y" construction.

Do not remove every "may", "associated" or "inferred" mechanically: they can define the type of result. Likewise, a non-significant estimate is not evidence of no effect. Report it once in the right place without making every paragraph apologetic.

Check the abstract ending and cover letter specifically for repeated negative conclusions. State the supported biological finding positively; do not finish both documents with a catalogue of untested mechanisms. If a statistical or source qualification is already clear in the relevant Results or Methods sentence, repeat it only when needed to prevent a new overclaim. A concise cover letter need not enumerate every non-significant secondary comparison; the manuscript must still report results faithfully. This is a placement decision, not permission to conceal a contradiction.

## Scope Of Integration

Reorder paragraphs, consolidate duplicate discussion and improve headings. Update all figure/table callouts and captions after any authorized reordering. Record the old/new mapping in working notes, not as internal language in final captions.

Changing a primary analysis, covariate, sampling window, statistical family or eligibility threshold is an analysis change. Ask before rerunning. If existing findings require a narrower interpretation, edit the interpretation rather than inventing a better result. Missing author facts remain questions in working notes, not fabricated statements or unnoticed blanks in final upload files.
